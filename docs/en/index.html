What this is about

Worked out, not summarized

From their own work

Every episode starts with something the two of them did themselves. An agent that completed AI training courses including the exams without being asked and brought the certificates back. A loop that opened 4,800 windows over one weekend. A knowledge store that pulls up old blood test results in seconds during a doctor's appointment.

Including when it goes wrong

An agent that quietly drops a failed test case and reports that all ten are green. A weekend loop with no stop condition that opens 4,800 windows and in the end says itself that it cannot repair this either. Moments like that do not get cut out, they get taken apart.

With numbers instead of adjectives

What hardware a model with 2.8 trillion parameters needs. What a language model can still manage on an eight-dollar chip. What API costs a simulated strategy workshop runs up. The math is in the episode, not in a marketing text.

Seen from here

The AI Act, GDPR and the question of when you count as a deployer and no longer just a user. What happens when law firms build their text analysis around a model that gets blocked for non-US citizens shortly afterwards. Topics that English-language shows do not cover this way.

By topic

Where the episodes converge

  • 16AI AgentsAgents act instead of only answering. These episodes cover agent harnesses, MCP as an interface, multi-agent setups and the question of who actually leads them.
  • 3AI SecurityPrompt Injection, voice cloning, exploits built from security patches: what happens when you give a model permissions, and what attacks on it look like in practice.
  • 8Automation and ToolsNotion, n8n, workflows and the tools behind them. Episodes about how a note turns into a ticket, a slide and a post.
  • 2Law and RegulationAI Act, data protection and liability. Who answers for what an agent does, and what data protection actually allows.
  • 7RoboticsAI gets a body. Humanoids, teleoperation and the distance between the promo video and the living room.
  • 7Knowledge ManagementSecond Brain, RAG, ontologies and portable Skills. How knowledge stays outside the model and is still findable.
  • 10Software DevelopmentVibe coding, spec-driven development and agents that commit. What has actually changed in day-to-day development work.
  • 5Models and ProvidersWho builds which model, what it costs and who is allowed to use it. From open weights out of China through token bills to the day a model was blocked for non-US citizens and law firms had already built their text analysis on it.
  • 7Interfaces and InteractionHow to work with something that does not respond like a program. Waiting time as a design question, voice instead of screen, the browser as an agent, and the question of how one person keeps track of several agents at the same time.
  • 6Leadership and WorkWhat happens to organizations when agents become part of the team. Hierarchies, responsibility and the question of where people starting their careers still learn.
